# A2

Writing at A2: who reads it, the page's language, its tags, and what it is measured against.
**`src/data/navigation.ts` is the truth about what exists.** The other rungs: [`a1.md`](a1.md),
[`b1.md`](b1.md), [`b2.md`](b2.md).

**A2 is written first** (#74), so the briefs in `.claude/agents/` already describe an A2 page; this
file holds only what is specific to the rung.

## Who reads it

**The learner** who has the basics and is building the past and the pronouns. **The heritage
speaker is not an A2** (#13): their pages (`orthographe`, `astuces`, `conjugaison`) answer to
literacy, mostly with no level.

## The page explains in French and in Spanish (#102)

Each section has a Spanish twin (`lesson-author.md`, « Two languages »); what follows holds for the
French body, and the Spanish one is as plain.

- **The explanation is easier than the French it teaches** — the first thing
  `content-proofreader` hunts. Présent, passé composé, imparfait, futur proche; no subjunctive, no
  literary tenses, no metalanguage beyond *verbe, sujet, adjectif, accord*.
- **The `.rule` box is where it fails**: no `dont`, no `lequel`, nothing three clauses long.
- **False friends defined, wrong-then-right, no Spanish printed as the cause of a mistake** —
  AGENTS.md §1. Spanish shown as material (a gloss, the Spanish behind a mistake) is A1's (#85)
  and a `traduction` source's (#55), nothing else's.

## How a page is tagged

- **`level: "A2"`** — listed to whoever keeps A2 in view (#86). Put it in the A2 parcours, in
  teaching order (`src/data/parcours/a2.ts`, #88); so is every A2 set of a page whose floor is
  lower. Left off, the import fails.
- **A page with `sets` writes them out** — `["A2", "B1"]`, or `["A1", "A2", "B1"]` on a literary
  work (#87, #92); adding or removing `sets` is a data migration (AGENTS.md §8). A visitor with no
  parcours sees the A2 set.
- **A DELF épreuve is `level: "A2"`**, and ends the A2 parcours as its `exam` (#89): a B1
  candidate sits the B1 paper.
- **The six points whose floor is A2 have no A1 twin to write** (#72); everything else shared with
  A1 owes one — both lists are in [`a1.md`](a1.md).
- **In a chapter, A2 pages sit after A1's and before B1's** — the chapter's order is the course's
  (#72).

## What it is measured against

**DELF A2 is what "done" means** (#15), from the same *Inventaire* Annexe E as A1 (its A2 page,
pp. 57–58 of the document); the source and the reasoning are in [`a1.md`](a1.md), « The source ».
**The labels below are ours**, shortened — the document is cited, never copied (§9b, #89). **The
map goes stale the moment a page lands**: re-check a line before relying on it, and when #89's
syllabus data is built, this map moves there.

Marked **·** where a page written at A2 — its `level`, or its A2 set — covers the point, *(part)*
when it covers only some of it. A page below A2 or with no level is named in brackets when it
carries the point instead. **Words offered in a role-play's cloud count as part, never whole**: a
scene uses a form, it does not teach it.

## GRAMMAIRE — twenty-nine points

**Covered at A2**: le futur proche **·** (`gram-futur-proche`) · le passé composé avec *avoir* et
*être* **·** (`gram-passe-compose`, `astuce-etre-ou-avoir`, `ex-etre-ou-avoir`) and the agreement
of its participle with *être* **·** (`gram-passe-compose`) · l'imparfait **·** (`gram-imparfait`) ·
l'alternance passé composé / imparfait **·** (`gram-pc-ou-imparfait`, `ex-raconter-ses-vacances`) ·
la négation *ne… plus, rien, personne* **·** (`gram-negation`, `astuce-pas-de`) · les pronoms COD
et COI **·** (`gram-pronoms-cod-coi`, `trad-hier-dans-la-rue`) · l'opposition, la cause, la
conséquence, les articulateurs chronologiques **·** (`gram-connecteurs`, `ex-mais-donc-parce-que`).

**In part**: le présent *(the verb sheets, no level; `ex-terminaisons`; « être en train de » is
missing)* · le futur simple and l'impératif *(their forms only: the verb sheets and
`ex-terminaisons`; no page says when to use them)* · le passé récent *(missing beside the passé
composé)* · les verbes modaux + infinitif *(the sheets of `pouvoir`, `vouloir`, `devoir`; no page
on the construction)* · le conditionnel de politesse *(« je voudrais » explained at A1,
`conv-faire-des-achats`; the tense is B1's `gram-conditionnel-present`)* · les verbes pronominaux
*(at the passé composé only, `gram-passe-compose`)* · les adverbes de quantité *(`gram-articles-partitifs`:
beaucoup, peu, assez de)* · les prépositions de lieu *(`astuce-a-en-au-aux` for towns and
countries; `conv-demander-son-chemin`'s cloud for the rest)* · les indicateurs de temps *(scattered;
no page)* · les articulateurs *(`gram-connecteurs`; « en plus, encore » are not there)*.

**Missing**: l'exclamation (*quel… !, que… !*) · les verbes prépositionnels (*arrêter de, penser à*)
· les adjectifs, place et accord · *tout, toute, tous, toutes* · la comparaison et le superlatif ·
les pronoms toniques et possessifs · *on* et ses trois sens · les pronoms *en* et *y* · les pronoms
relatifs *qui, que, où* · le but (*pour* + nom ou infinitif). **Three of these have their floor at
A2** — la comparaison, *en* / *y*, les relatifs — so they have no A1 twin to wait for; the other
three of [`a1.md`](a1.md)'s « six whose floor is A2 » are written.

## THÈMES DE VOCABULAIRE — thirteen, four at A2

**At A2**: le travail **·** (`voc-travail`) · les vacances et les voyages **·** (`voc-vacances`) ·

`voc-heure`, `voc-jours-et-date`, `voc-opinion` and `voc-critique` serve functions (1, 11, 30), not
themes.

## DISCOURS AND SOCIO-CULTUREL — seven

*Discours*: ask someone to repeat **·** (`conv-demander-son-chemin`) · open and close a
conversation *(part: the role-plays' steps)* · check one was understood —. *Socio-culturel*:
*tu* or *vous* *(A1: `astuce-tu-ou-vous`)* · the formulas for inviting, thanking, apologising
*(part: thanking and apologising at A1, `astuce-tu-ou-vous`; inviting nowhere)* · the friendly
letter and the e-mail — *(`delf-a2-production-ecrite` asks for one and nothing teaches it)* · the
telephone's rituals **·** (`conv-rendez-vous-medecin`).

## Order of work

1. **`conversation`, the social half**: an invitation (3, with the formulas of the socio-culturel
   list), planning an outing (8, 9), asking for help (6), describing someone (23). Each is a scene
   a learner meets in the first month abroad, and none is written.
2. **The friendly letter or e-mail** — a `traduction` or a page of its own: the A2 épreuve asks for
   one (`delf-a2-production-ecrite`), and nothing in the course prepares it.
3. **The three points whose floor is A2**: la comparaison, *en* / *y*, les relatifs *qui, que, où*.
   One page each; the comparative is already in two role-play clouds, waiting for its rule.
4. **`vocabulaire`**: les personnes (physique, caractère), la santé, les loisirs — the themes the
   scenes above need.
5. **Short pages for the forms already in the sheets**: when to use the impératif and the futur
   simple, and the passé récent beside the passé composé.

**The A2 parcours places each new page** in the étape of the function it serves, or in a new one
— for the social half, an étape of its own between « Montrer, choisir, commander » and the past.
