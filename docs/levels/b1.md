# B1

Writing at B1: what B1 is here, the page's language, its tags, and what it is measured against.
**`src/data/navigation.ts` is the truth about what exists.** The other rungs: [`a1.md`](a1.md),
[`a2.md`](a2.md), [`b2.md`](b2.md).

**B1 is offered while being written** (#74). A learner who wants the A2 pages too keeps A2 in view
(#86).

## What B1 material is

Two kinds, and nothing else:

- **A new point whose floor is B1** — a page with `level: "B1"`, in the B1 parcours after what it
  builds on (#88). The first ones are in `grammaire`; count them in the
  manifest.
- **A harder set on a page with no floor** — the B1 bank of a drill, the B1 questions of a
  `lecture` text (`sets`, #68, #87). How: `lesson-author.md` (« The harder set asks more of the
  same page »). **Read the A2 set before writing the B1 one, every time.**
- **The B1 body of a literary work** (#92) — `b1.tsx` beside `a2.tsx` on the same page: the
  passage without cuts, or its companion text, with its own questions.

**Never a B1 copy of an A2 page** (#86): the A2 page is one tick of the view away.

**Not every text can carry a B1 set honestly** — past a point a harder question is trivia. List the
sets a page can actually serve.

## The page explains in French and in Spanish (#102)

Everything in [`a2.md`](a2.md) holds. **The explanation may use what A2 taught, never the point
on the page**: a page on *ce dont* may not explain itself with *dont*.

## How a page is tagged

- **`level: "B1"`** — listed to whoever keeps B1 in view (#86).
- **A page with `sets` lists exactly the levels it has material for** — `["A2", "B1"]`, or
  `["A1", "A2", "B1"]` on a literary work (#92). **Each set goes in its level's parcours**: a B1
  set added to a page is a line in `src/data/parcours/b1.ts` too, or the import fails (#88).
  Adding a B1 set to a page that has none is a key change (AGENTS.md §8).
- **A DELF épreuve is `level: "B1"`**, and ends the B1 parcours as its `exam` (#89).
- **A `delf` descriptor is per level** on a page with `sets` — `{ A2: '…', B1: '…' }` — because it
  says what that level's questions check.

## What it is measured against

**DELF B1** (#15), from the same *Inventaire* Annexe E as A1 (its B1 page, p. 59 of the
document); the source and the reasoning are in [`a1.md`](a1.md), « The source ». **The labels below
are ours**, shortened — the document is cited, never copied (§9b, #89). **The map goes stale the

Marked **·** where a page written at B1 — its `level`, or its B1 set — covers the point, *(part)*
when it covers only some of it. The last column names the page below B1 (A2, or A1) that already
teaches the language at its own floor: a B1 page there **asks more of the same function**, it does not repeat
the A2 one (#86).

## GRAMMAIRE — thirty points

**Written at B1**: le plus-que-parfait **·** (`gram-plus-que-parfait`) · le conditionnel présent
**·**, et dans le souhait et l'hypothèse **·** *(part, `gram-conditionnel-present`)* · l'hypothèse
avec *si* **·** *(part: si + imparfait → conditionnel; si + présent reste)* · la mise en relief,
*ce qui / ce que / ce dont… c'est* **·** (`gram-ce-qui-ce-que`) · le relatif *dont* *(part: « ce
dont » seulement)*.

**At A2, which B1 spirals on**: l'alternance passé composé / imparfait and the tenses of a story
(`gram-pc-ou-imparfait`, `gram-imparfait`, `gram-passe-compose`) · la négation, A2's exponents only
(`gram-negation`; *ne… que, aucun, ni… ni* are B1's) · la cause et la concession, A2's words only
(`gram-connecteurs`: *comme*, *pourtant*).

**Missing**: *si* + imparfait for a proposal (*et si on sortait ?*) · le subjonctif présent, after
*il faut que* and the verbs of feeling, and its line with the indicative (*j'espère que / je
souhaite que*) · le passif · le gérondif · l'accord du participe avec un COD placé avant · le
discours indirect au présent · les verbes prépositionnels (*penser à, arrêter de*) · la
nominalisation · les adverbes de quantité et de manière (*environ, autant, prudemment*) · les
prépositions de lieu *par, en* · le temps du récit (*autrefois, la veille, le lendemain, dès que*)
· deux pronoms ensemble (*je le lui donne*) · les pronoms indéfinis, *en* et *y* after a verb with
*à* or *de* · les relatifs composés (*auquel, duquel*) · l'opposition (*alors que, au contraire*) ·
le but (*pour que, afin de*) · la cause (*car, puisque, grâce à*) · la conséquence (*c'est
pourquoi, par conséquent*) · la concession (*bien que, malgré, cependant*) · les articulateurs
(*également, finalement*).

`gram-article-disparait` is not in the inventory: it answers the reader (an article a Spanish
speaker writes where French drops it), which is reason enough (#13).

## DISCOURS AND SOCIO-CULTUREL — eleven, none covered

*Discours*: open and close a conversation, ask for the floor, check one was understood, start and
end a discussion, change the subject, repeat to check, introduce a theme, refer back (*le premier,
le dernier, le suivant*), conclude (*pour terminer, enfin*). *Socio-culturel*: the formal or
business letter, and speaking in a meeting. **They are a role-play's furniture** (#57): a B1 scene's
word cloud is where they go, not a page of their own.

## Order of work

1. **`conversation` first**: B1 has no scene, and the gate is the FONCTIONS list. The functions
   with A2 language already in place cost least: **se plaindre** (28, on `voc-critique`), **un
   entretien** (20, the scene `lect-entretien-embauche` reads), **donner un conseil** and **poser
   un problème** (8, 25), **téléphoner** (3). Each level gets its own scene (AGENTS.md §7).
2. **The grammar those scenes need**: the subjunctive after *il faut que* and the verbs of feeling
   (12, 29), the indirect speech (16), B1's connecteurs — cause, consequence, concession, purpose (5,
   23, 24). One page each, in the B1 parcours before the scene that uses it.
3. **`vocabulaire`**: les sentiments, la santé, les médias — three of the seven themes with nothing at
   any level, and the three the functions above lean on.
4. **A B1 épreuve** in `delf` (#78, #82), the B1 parcours's `exam` (#89) — last, since it asks for
   everything above.

**The B1 parcours follows this map** (`src/data/parcours/b1.ts`): its étapes are named after the
functions above, and a new page joins the étape of the function it serves.
